# About these games

> **This file is the source of the "About these games" page.** It is shown in the game (a screen reached from the menu) and on the website (`/about`). It is the one place where the game explains where its rules come from, so individual modes carry no "adapted", "provisional" or "custom" labels. Draft in the author's voice; the author edits the words, and the game and site copy are kept identical to this file.

## The page

### These are childhood games

I grew up playing marble games on bare ground: a line drawn with a stick, a small hole, a handful of marbles, and no rulebook. The rules lived in our heads and changed a little from street to street. I made this game to bring those afternoons back.

### Where the rules come from

Nothing here is invented from nothing. Each mode starts with a game people really played:

- **Kep** is the toss at the hole that decided who went first.
- **Batto** is the three-hole race as I remember it: along the holes and back, hitting a rival's marble to earn another shot.
- **Guli Khela** is the game of the line, the hole, the stake, and the marble you must not touch. It follows a written Bangla account of the _jalla_, the _kep_, the _dan_, the _baad_ marble and the fine, and it agrees with the short description in Banglapedia.
- **Quick Dan** is one round of Guli Khela, for when you have a few minutes.

I use the words those accounts use. Where you grew up, the line, the hole or the game itself may have another name.

### What I changed

I changed some rules so the games work well on a phone. These are my decisions, not the tradition's:

- The game scatters the marbles around the hole for you.
- A marble that leaves the play area is put back where it left.
- Bonus shots and turns have limits, so a game always ends.
- In Batto, a marble that finishes the route leaves the ground, and a first toss that lands in the first hole counts as reaching it.
- The number of marbles in a stake, the size of a fine, and the number of rounds are set to make a good game on a small screen.
- You can play against computer players, and you can surrender.

### What this is not

It is not an official or complete rulebook, and it does not claim to be how the game was played everywhere. If you remember it differently, you are not wrong. Your way might be the one I never saw.

### Tell me how you played

If you know these games, I would like to hear how you played them: the names you used, the distances, what happened when someone missed. I will keep learning from you, and the rules will change as I do.

## Where it appears

- **In the game:** a screen "About these games" linked from the menu (a text action, not a button), and from the mode list as a small "About" link. It is shown once, after the first mode is chosen, and is always reachable from the menu. It is plain text with the same layout as the Settings screen.
- **On the website:** `/about`, linked from the header and the footer. The landing page's note links to it.
- **In the store listing and release notes:** one sentence and a link: "Inspired by the traditional marble games of Bangladesh. The rules were remembered and modified by the author; read how on the About page."

## Rules for using it

- Mode screens, mode rows and the HUD carry **no authenticity labels** (no "adapted", "provisional", "traditional", "authentic" or "official"). They state the rule, not its history.
- Nothing in the game, the site or the store may call a mode "the official rules", "the authentic game" or "how it is really played".
- Every change to a mode's rules updates this page's "What I changed" list in the same change.
- When someone contributes how they played, record who and where, with their permission, in [README_GAME_RULES.md](README_GAME_RULES.md) and thank them on this page.
- Respect the people and places the games come from: no stock "village" imagery or recordings to imply authenticity.
